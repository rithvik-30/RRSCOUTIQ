## 3. Section B: Investigation of Phase 4.3 vs Phase 4.4 Ranking Differences

Phase 4.3 documented experimental prototype rankings, whereas Phase 4.4 generated the formal shadow-mode artifacts. An investigation into the observed rank shifts revealed the exact mathematical and operational causes.

### 3.1 Root Cause Analysis: Walk-Forward Par vs 5-Fold Cross Validation Par
1. **The Phase 4.3 Prototype Formulation:**
   - In `scratch/phase4_3_ranking_integrity_audit.py`, venue par was generated using 5-fold cross validation (`venue_par_fold_safe`) across the entire 2022–2026 dataset.
   - While fold-safe within the cross-validation partition, this allowed matches from 2025 and 2026 to inform the venue par of fixtures in 2022 and 2023.
2. **The Phase 4.4 Shadow Pipeline Implementation:**
   - Section 7 of the contract strictly specified a **strictly chronological walk-forward par estimator** (`ChronologicalVenueParEstimator`).
   - For every match on date $T$, par was estimated using strictly completed matches played before $T$.
   - This adjusted venue par by $1.5$ to $3.5$ runs in the earlier seasons (2022–2023), subtly shifting delivery-level BPS values around the $60.0$ boundary.

### 3.2 Detailed Player-Level Reconciliation
- **Batters: Yashasvi Jaiswal vs Travis Head**
  - **Travis Head:** Under walk-forward par, SRH's aggressive 2024 early matches were calibrated against strictly prior venue par, raising Head's qualifying pressure deliveries from $154$ (in Phase 4.3) to $245$ balls (in Phase 4.4). With a higher exposure sample, his latent standard error dropped ($\overline{\text{SE}} = 0.0281$), elevating his conservative lower bound ($\text{LB90} = \text{mean\_val\_add} - 1.645 \cdot \text{SE}$) from $-1.83\%$ to $\mathbf{+0.65\%}$, moving him from Rank #7 to Rank #2.
  - **Yashasvi Jaiswal:** Pressure balls shifted from $330$ to $264$ balls. His Value Add remained elite ($+4.72\%$), producing $\text{LB90} = \mathbf{+0.44\%}$, moving him from Rank #2 to Rank #3. Both Head and Jaiswal remain firmly in the top 3 batters in world cricket.
  - **Qualified Count (104 vs 103):** 1 marginal batter with exactly 101 balls at BPS $\ge 60$ in Phase 4.3 fell to 98 balls in Phase 4.4, missing the 100-ball filter.

- **Bowlers: Jasprit Bumrah vs Sunil Narine**
  - In Phase 4.3: Narine was Rank #1 (51 spells, UB90 $+0.351$) and Bumrah was Rank #2 (73 spells, UB90 $+0.377$).
  - In Phase 4.4: Bumrah is Rank #1 (87 spells, UB90 $\mathbf{+0.387}$) and Narine is Rank #2 (63 spells, UB90 $\mathbf{+0.403}$).
  - **Magnitude of Difference:** The difference in conservative upper bound between Bumrah and Narine in Phase 4.4 is just $\mathbf{0.016\text{ RPO}}$ (less than 1 run per 60 overs!). Bumrah's additional 14 pressure spells reduced his shrinkage penalty, placing him at #1. Both remain virtually tied at the absolute apex of bowling impact.
  - **Qualified Count (102 vs 105):** 3 additional spinners (including Sikandar Raza and Akeal Hosein) crossed the 20-pressure-spell threshold under walk-forward par.

## 7. Section F: Verification of Expected Delivery Leverage

Expected delivery leverage was verified by testing whether the pre-delivery expectation correctly forecasts the actual realized win probability movement on the subsequent legal delivery:
$$L_t = \sum_o P(o \mid x_t) \left|\text{WP}(x_{t+1, o}) - \text{WP}(x_t)\right| \times 100$$
Realized delivery swing was calculated on consecutive legal chase transitions:
$$\Delta \text{WP}_t^{\text{realized}} = \left|\text{WP}_{t+1} - \text{WP}_t\right| \times 100$$
